Senior Thesis · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

The Role of Parvalbumin-Positive Interneurons in Mediating the Expression of Extinction Learning in the Hippocampus

Jingtong He · Advisor: Dr. Howard J. Gritton

Download full text (PDF) Open in new tab

Overview

Extinction is the suppression of a learned response when its previously associated outcome no longer occurs. Both original associative learning and subsequent extinction learning depend on the hippocampus formation in the medial temporal lobe.

This thesis studies how local inhibitory parvalbumin-positive (PV+) interneurons and excitatory populations in hippocampal CA1 changed through the process of new learning and subsequent extinction. Using dual-color calcium imaging in PV-tdTomato-Cre mice, I tracked PV+ and non-PV neurons across conditioning and extinction. PV+ ensembles reorganized transiently and specifically during early extinction learning.

Using Local Field Potential (LFP) recordings, I found that tone-onset 9-12 Hz LFP power built up gradually over extinction and was associated with learning.
